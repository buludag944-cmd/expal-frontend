Finding accommodation is often one of the most challenging parts of moving to Ireland. Rental demand can be high, particularly in Dublin, Cork, Galway and Limerick, so it helps to search across several platforms and respond quickly to suitable listings.

Here are some of the main websites to use.

1. Daft.ie

Daft.ie is Ireland’s largest property-listing website and is usually the first place to check. It includes:

Houses and apartments to rent
Rooms in shared accommodation
Student accommodation
Short- and long-term options

Create alerts for your preferred locations and price range because suitable properties can receive many enquiries quickly.

2. Rent.ie

Rent.ie lists houses, apartments, studios and rooms across Ireland. You can search by county, property type and monthly budget.

Some listings may also appear on Daft, but checking both platforms can help you avoid missing an opportunity.

3. MyHome.ie

MyHome.ie is another established Irish property platform. Although it is well known for properties for sale, it also has residential rental listings from landlords and letting agents.

4. Property.ie

Property.ie includes properties to rent throughout Ireland. It can be particularly useful when you want to expand your search beyond the biggest platforms or look in smaller towns.

5. Hosting Power

Hosting Power focuses on furnished private rooms in host homes. Options are available for students and professionals in Dublin, Cork, Galway, Limerick and other parts of Ireland.

This may suit newcomers who want accommodation arranged before arriving or who prefer to begin with a room while learning more about the local rental market.

Check the booking conditions, service charges, cancellation rules and what is included before confirming.

6. Local letting agents

Many estate and letting agencies advertise available properties on their own websites as well as on the larger platforms.

Search for “letting agents” followed by the town, neighbourhood or county where you want to live. Registering your interest directly with several agencies may help you hear about suitable properties.

Never assume that an individual is a legitimate agent simply because they use an agency’s name. Verify their details through the company’s official website.

7. University accommodation pages

If you are moving to Ireland to study, check your university or college’s official accommodation page.

Institutions may provide:

On-campus accommodation
Approved student residences
Private accommodation directories
Advice about renting locally
Scam warnings and tenant-support information

Student accommodation can fill quickly, so apply as soon as the relevant booking period opens.

8. Cost Rental providers

Cost Rental homes are intended for eligible households whose income is above the social-housing threshold but who may struggle with private-market rents.

Available homes may be advertised through:

Land Development Agency
Affordable Homes Ireland
Approved Housing Bodies such as Tuath, Clúid and Oaklee
Local-authority websites

Eligibility rules apply, and accommodation is limited. Applications are generally made for individual developments rather than through one permanent waiting list. Check the current requirements for every scheme before applying.

9. Facebook and community groups

Local Facebook groups can sometimes help you find rooms, house shares or people seeking new housemates. Search for groups connected to your city, university, profession or international community.

However, social-media advertisements require extra caution. Do not treat a profile, photograph, message or rental agreement as proof that an offer is genuine.

Important rental-safety tips

Before transferring money:

View the accommodation in person whenever possible.
Verify the identity and authority of the landlord or agent.
Read the rental agreement carefully.
Confirm exactly what is included in the rent.
Keep written records and receipts for every payment.
Never send money because someone is pressuring you to decide immediately.
Be cautious if the price looks unusually low.
Check current guidance from the Residential Tenancies Board.

The RTB specifically advises renters to be alert to scams, obtain written agreements and receipts, and avoid transferring money without viewing the property.
