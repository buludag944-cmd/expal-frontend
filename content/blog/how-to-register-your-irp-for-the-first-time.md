If you are moving to Ireland from outside the EU, UK or Switzerland and staying longer than 90 days, registering your immigration permission is one of your most important first tasks. Once you are registered, you receive an Irish Residence Permit (IRP) card. Here is how the process works.

## Who needs to register

You need to register if you are a national of a country outside the EU, UK or Switzerland and you are staying in Ireland for **more than 90 days** to work, study, live or join family.

Children under 16 do not need to register.

## The 90-day rule

The stamp you receive when you land tells you to register **within 90 days** of arrival. Book your appointment as soon as you arrive.

Appointments are released on a rolling 90-day basis and can be rescheduled. If no appointment is available within your 90 days, booking one within that period is enough to remain in the State until your appointment date.

## Step 1: Book your appointment online

Book through the Immigration Service [Customer Service Portal](https://portal.irishimmigration.ie). Create an account or log in, then choose a first-time registration appointment.

**Booking is free.** You only pay the registration fee at the appointment, once your registration is completed. Any third party selling appointments is not operating on behalf of the Immigration Service and has no special access to the booking system.

## Step 2: Prepare your documents

The documents you need depend on your type of permission (your "stamp"). Check the official [required documents list](https://www.irishimmigration.ie/required-documents/) for your category before your appointment.

If you don't have all your required documents or the fee with you, your registration can't be completed, so check the list twice.

## Step 3: Attend your appointment at Burgh Quay

All first-time registration appointments take place at the nationwide **Burgh Quay Registration Office, 13/14 Burgh Quay, Dublin 2, D02 XK70**, even if you live outside Dublin.

At the appointment:

- your documents are reviewed
- your photograph and fingerprints are taken
- your passport is stamped with the permission to reside you have been granted

Allow up to about 60 minutes for the visit.

## The fee

The standard registration fee is **€300 per person**, where it applies. You pay by credit or debit card at the appointment. Cash is not accepted at the registration office. Some categories of permission don't pay the fee, and you will be told the amount at the time of registration.

## When your IRP card arrives

Your IRP card is posted to the home address you gave, usually within about **15 business days** (excluding weekends and public holidays). You can't collect the card from the office, so make sure your address and Eircode are correct.

## After first registration

Renewals and stamp changes are done **online only**, not in person. Note your card's expiry date and start your renewal in good time.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Waiting too long to book. Book in your first days in Ireland.
- Paying a website or agent for an appointment. Booking is free on the official portal.
- Bringing the wrong documents for your stamp type.
- Bringing cash. Only cards are accepted.
- Giving an address where you won't be living when the card is posted.

## FAQ

**Do I have to travel to Dublin to register for the first time?**
Yes. Burgh Quay in Dublin is the nationwide office for all first-time registration appointments. Renewals are done online.

**Is there a fee to book an appointment?**
No. Booking is free. The registration fee is paid only at the appointment.

**How long does the IRP card take to arrive?**
Usually about 15 business days, by post to your home address.
