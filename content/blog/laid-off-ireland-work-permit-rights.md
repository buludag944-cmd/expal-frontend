If you hold a Critical Skills Employment Permit (CSEP) or General Employment Permit (GEP) and you've just been made redundant, you have two clocks running: 28 days to tell the government, and six months to find your next job. Miss the first and the second gets much harder.
The good news: losing your job does not mean losing your right to be in Ireland overnight. You also keep the same employment rights as any Irish or EU worker — redundancy pay, notice, holiday pay and protection from unfair dismissal don't depend on your passport.

This guide walks through what to do, what you're owed, and who to call. It's general information, not legal advice — rules change, so always confirm your own case with the organisations listed below.
Step one: notify DETE within 28 days

Your most important task is to send the Redundancy Notification Form to the Employment Permits Section of the Department of Enterprise, Tourism and Employment (DETE) within four weeks of your redundancy date. This applies to both CSEP and GEP holders.

1. Download the form from the DETE redundancy page.
2. Fill it in and send it to the Employment Permits Section, Earlsfort Centre, Lower Hatch Street, Dublin 2. The Migrant Rights Centre Ireland recommends registered post.
3. Keep the tracking number and a copy of the form. If anyone later questions whether you notified on time, that receipt is your proof.
4. Ask your employer for a letter confirming the redundancy and your last day. Your future permit application will need it.
Why it matters: notifying on time is what unlocks the protections in the next section. Don't wait until your notice period ends to start — the clock runs from the redundancy date.

Your six-month window

Once you've notified DETE, you have up to six months from the redundancy date to find a new job and apply for a new employment permit. You can stay in Ireland during this period on your existing permission.
What the window gives you:

• No Labour Market Needs Test. Normally a new GEP employer must advertise the role for 28 days first. After a notified redundancy, that step is waived — a real selling point when you talk to employers.
• Protection if your role became ineligible. If your occupation has since been moved onto the ineligible list, or off the Critical Skills list, DETE will still consider the same permit type if a different employer offers you the same job.
• A wider field. You can look for work in any occupation that's eligible for a permit, not only your old one.
Remember that you cannot start work for a new employer until the new permit is granted. Factor processing times into your plans and tell recruiters upfront that you'll need sponsorship.
If your IRP card expires during the six months, contact Immigration Service Delivery (ISD) early rather than letting it lapse.
If six months pass without a new job, your options narrow. ISD says you can contact them about your immigration permission, and the Reactivation Employment Permit scheme may grant a temporary six-month Stamp 1 so you can apply for a permit. If you leave Ireland, you can still apply for a new permit later if you get a job offer.

What your employer owes you

Your permit status doesn't reduce your employment rights. Check each of these before you sign anything.
Statutory redundancy pay. If you've worked continuously for the same employer for at least 104 weeks (two years), you're entitled to two weeks' pay per year of service plus one bonus week. Weekly pay is capped at €600 for the calculation, and the payment is tax-free. Example: 4 years of service = (4 × 2) + 1 = 9 weeks × €600 = €5,400. If your employer is insolvent, the Department of Social Protection's Redundancy Payment Scheme can pay instead.

Notice. Your employer must give written notice of redundancy at least two weeks before dismissal. Separately, you're owed minimum notice based on your service (or more if your contract says so):

• 13 weeks to 2 years of service: 1 week
• 2 to 5 years: 2 weeks
• 5 to 10 years: 4 weeks
• 10 to 15 years: 6 weeks
• 15 years or more: 8 weeks

Holiday pay. Any annual leave you've earned but not taken must be paid out in your final wages.

Final pay and paperwork. Expect your last payslip, any outstanding wages or commission, and your final payslip details on Revenue's myAccount.
Is it really a redundancy? A genuine redundancy means your role no longer exists and you aren't replaced. If you suspect you were chosen unfairly, or replaced soon after, you may have an unfair dismissal claim. Complaints go to the Workplace Relations Commission, generally within six months of dismissal.

Severance packages. Many employers, especially in tech, offer more than the statutory minimum. Read any settlement agreement carefully — signing it usually means giving up the right to bring claims later. You're entitled to take time and get advice first.

Money, tax and social welfare

Jobseeker's Benefit. This payment is based on your PRSI contributions, not your nationality, so some permit holders who have paid enough PRSI can qualify. Eligibility depends on your contribution record and your immigration permission, so ask at your local Intreo centre before assuming either way. Means-tested payments such as Jobseeker's Allowance are usually much harder to access on a work permit.

Tax on severance. Statutory redundancy is tax-free. Extra (ex-gratia) payments above it may be partly tax-free under Revenue's exemptions, and the rest is taxed. Check Revenue's guidance on lump sum payments at revenue.ie, or ask your payroll team how it was calculated.
Tax refund. Losing your job mid-year often means you've overpaid income tax. Once you're out of work, you can claim it back through Revenue's myAccount.

Where to get help
Most of these services are free. Start with Citizens Information for a general picture, then go to the specialist for your issue.

• DETE Employment Permits Section (free): redundancy notification and new permit applications.

• Immigration Service Delivery (ISD) (free): your IRP and Stamp, your permission after six months, and the Reactivation scheme.

• Citizens Information (free): plain-language guidance on redundancy, welfare and immigration, by phone or at walk-in centres.

• Workplace Relations Commission (WRC) (free): unpaid redundancy, notice or holiday pay, and unfair dismissal complaints.

• Migrant Rights Centre Ireland (MRCI) (free): confidential support for migrant workers on permit and employment issues.

• Immigrant Council of Ireland (free): immigration information and legal support.

• FLAC (free): legal information phone line and legal advice clinics.

• Intreo (free): Jobseeker's Benefit, the Redundancy Payment Scheme and job search support.

• Your trade union (membership): representation and help negotiating your package, if you're a member.

• An employment or immigration solicitor (paid): reviewing a settlement agreement or handling a complex case.

Tip: if your employer offers to pay for legal advice on a settlement agreement, take it — many do.

Your checklist
• Get written confirmation of the redundancy and your last day
• Send the Redundancy Notification Form to DETE by registered post within 28 days
• Keep the tracking number and a copy of the form
• Note the date six months from your redundancy
• Check your IRP expiry date
• Check redundancy pay, notice and holiday pay against your contract
• Get advice before signing any settlement agreement
• Ask Intreo about Jobseeker's Benefit
• Claim any tax refund through Revenue myAccount
• Tell recruiters you need a permit, and that the Labour Market Needs Test is waived.

Redundancy is stressful, and doubly so when your right to stay is tied to your job. But the system does give you time and protection — as long as you act quickly on that first form.
